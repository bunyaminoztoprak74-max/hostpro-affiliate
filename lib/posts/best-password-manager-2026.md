## What Matters More Than Price

Use a long, unique master passphrase; enable the strongest MFA available; store recovery codes offline; review emergency access; and keep devices patched. Before committing, test export and recovery with non-critical data. A vault you cannot restore is not resilient.

## Passkeys

Passkeys resist phishing by binding authentication to the legitimate site. Support is expanding, but recovery and cross-platform sharing still vary. Choose a manager that can store both passkeys and conventional credentials while preserving an exit path.

## How We Rank Password Managers

Our order emphasizes security design, recovery, reliable cross-platform autofill, export options and sharing controls. A low promotional price cannot compensate for an untested recovery path or a vault that fails on a required device.

We do not treat “zero knowledge” as a complete guarantee. Endpoint malware, phishing, malicious extensions, weak account email security and exposed recovery methods remain important risks.

## Recovery and Exit Plan

Before importing important credentials:

1. Protect the account email with separate MFA.
2. Store recovery codes offline.
3. Add several non-critical test records.
4. Test account recovery without risking the main vault.
5. Export test data and confirm the format is usable.
6. Delete unencrypted export files after testing.

Business buyers should also test provisioning, offboarding, audit logs, policy enforcement and administrator recovery.

## Free vs Paid Password Managers

Free tiers can be sufficient for one user when they support every required device. Paid plans usually add family sharing, emergency access, advanced reports, aliases, integrated 2FA or business controls.

Do not buy a long plan until the manager works on your desktop, primary browser and phone. Also compare the renewal amount rather than only the introductory discount.
