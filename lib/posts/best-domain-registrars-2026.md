## 1. Cloudflare Registrar — Best Overall Value

**Rating: 4.8/5**

Cloudflare Registrar only sells domains you already have DNS pointed at Cloudflare for, and it charges exactly the ICANN wholesale rate — no markup, ever, on purchase, renewal, or transfer. For a `.com`, that's typically around $9-10/year for the life of the domain, with no surprise renewal jump.

**Why it stands out:**
- **No markup at any point** — purchase, renewal, and transfer all cost the same wholesale rate
- **Free WHOIS privacy** included permanently
- **Fastest DNS network** in independent benchmarks (Cloudflare's own 1.1.1.1 infrastructure)
- **No upsells** — no SSL certificate offers, no "premium" domain add-ons pushed at checkout

**The catch:** You must already be using Cloudflare's free DNS/CDN service for the domain, and Cloudflare doesn't sell every TLD. It's the best option for technically comfortable site owners who don't need hand-holding.

## 2. Namecheap — Best Standalone Registrar

**Rating: 4.6/5**

Namecheap remains the most consistently recommended standalone registrar. Pricing is transparent, WHOIS privacy is free for the life of the domain, and the support team is responsive without aggressive upselling. For a full breakdown of their hosting side too, see our [Namecheap review](/blog/namecheap-hosting-review-2026).

**Strengths:**
- Free WHOIS privacy (permanent, not a first-year trial)
- Clean dashboard with bulk management tools for agencies running multiple domains
- Reasonable renewal pricing compared to GoDaddy/Bluehost
- 2-factor authentication and domain lock included free

**Weaknesses:** Not the cheapest first-year price, and their hosting upsells during checkout can be persistent if you're not paying attention.

## 3. Hostinger — Best for Beginners Bundling Hosting + Domain

**Rating: 4.5/5**

If you're buying hosting and a domain together — the most common scenario for a first website — [Hostinger](https://www.hostinger.com/web-hosting?REFERRALCODE=OFMBZTOPRZSU) frequently includes a free domain for the first year with annual hosting plans. See our full [Hostinger review](/blog/hostinger-review-2026) for hosting performance details.

**Why beginners should consider it:**
- Domain + hosting in one dashboard — one login, one invoice
- Free domain for year one bundled with most annual plans

- Renewal pricing (~$14.99/year for .com) is mid-pack, not predatory

**Trade-off:** Bundling means your domain and hosting live in the same account. If you ever switch hosts, you'll want to either transfer the domain out or keep the Hostinger account active just for DNS.

## 4. Porkbun — Best for Unusual TLDs and Developers

**Rating: 4.4/5**

Porkbun has built a loyal following among developers for consistently low pricing across both common and unusual TLDs (.dev, .io, .app), a clean API, and no-markup transfers. WHOIS privacy is free and permanent.

**Strengths:** Competitive pricing across almost every TLD, clean UI, solid API for programmatic DNS management, free privacy and free SSL certificate included with every domain.

**Weaknesses:** Smaller company than GoDaddy/Namecheap, so support responsiveness can vary. Newer TLDs sometimes have stricter registration requirements.

## 5. Google Domains Successor (Squarespace Domains) — Declining Value

**Rating: 3.6/5**

After Google exited the domain registrar business, its domain portfolio moved to Squarespace. Existing customers report the transition has been rocky, with fewer developer-friendly tools than Google Domains originally offered and increased upsell pressure toward Squarespace's website builder. New buyers have better options above.

## 6. GoDaddy — Avoid Unless Heavily Discounted

**Rating: 2.9/5**

GoDaddy remains the most recognizable domain brand, which is exactly why it can get away with the industry's most aggressive renewal pricing and checkout upsells (SSL certificates, "premium" DNS, website builder add-ons all pushed hard at purchase). A $0.99 first-year `.com` routinely renews at $20-25/year — a 20-25x increase.

**Only use GoDaddy if:** a domain is bundled free with a promotion you're already taking advantage of elsewhere, and even then, consider transferring it to Namecheap or Cloudflare once the free period ends.

## Domain + Hosting: Should You Bundle or Split?

Bundling a domain with your hosting account is convenient, but it creates a single point of failure. If your host has a billing dispute, security incident, or extended downtime, an intertwined domain/hosting account complicates fixing it quickly. Our general recommendation:

- **Beginners with one site:** Bundle is fine. Use [Hostinger](/blog/hostinger-review-2026) or whichever host you choose, and keep the login credentials backed up somewhere safe.
- **Anyone running a business-critical site:** Register the domain separately (Cloudflare or Namecheap) and point DNS to your host. This is standard practice among agencies and matches what we recommend in our [best web hosting 2026](/blog/best-web-hosting-2026) guide.
- **Agencies managing client domains:** Namecheap's bulk tools or Cloudflare for Teams make multi-domain management significantly easier than juggling logins across multiple hosting providers.
