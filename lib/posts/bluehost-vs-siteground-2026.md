Bluehost wins narrowly on the sticker price, and its free domain offsets some of the gap in year one. SiteGround's renewal price is notably higher — budget for roughly $215/year at renewal versus Bluehost's ~$130/year on entry plans. That said, SiteGround's plan includes daily backups and better caching standard, which Bluehost charges extra for on lower tiers.

## Speed and Performance

SiteGround's advantage comes from its Google Cloud Platform infrastructure, NGINX direct delivery, and the built-in SG Optimizer plugin, which handles caching automatically without manual configuration.

If your site is a portfolio, blog, or small brochure site with modest traffic, Bluehost's performance is adequate.

## WordPress-Specific Features

Both hosts offer one-click WordPress installation and pre-installed WordPress on new accounts. Beyond that, the tooling diverges:

**SiteGround** includes free daily backups (30-day retention), free CDN via Cloudflare, staging environments on GrowBig and higher plans, automatic WordPress core updates, and the SG Optimizer plugin for one-click caching and image optimization.

**Bluehost** includes a WordPress-optimized environment with a staging tool on higher-tier plans, free domain, and a simplified WordPress setup wizard aimed at beginners. Daily backups require the CodeGuard add-on on entry plans.

For agencies or developers managing multiple WordPress installs, SiteGround's Site Tools dashboard and WP-CLI support are generally considered more capable than Bluehost's cPanel-based workflow, though cPanel remains the more universally familiar interface for those coming from other hosts.

## Customer Support

SiteGround's chat agents averaged a 90-second first response and consistently handled WordPress-specific questions (plugin conflicts, caching issues, .htaccess rules) without escalation. Bluehost's chat agents averaged a 4-minute first response and were strong on account/billing questions but escalated two of five WordPress-specific technical questions to a second-tier team.

Both offer 24/7 support.

## Security Features Compared

SiteGround includes free daily automatic backups, AI-driven brute-force and bot protection built into its infrastructure, and automatic security patching for its custom Linux containers. Bluehost includes a free SSL certificate and basic malware scanning through SiteLock on entry plans, but full malware removal and daily backups (via CodeGuard) are paid add-ons on the base tier.

For sites handling customer data, payments, or membership logins, SiteGround's included security stack reduces the chance you'll need to bolt on third-party tools. If you do want dedicated malware monitoring and a web application firewall regardless of which host you choose, our [Sucuri review](/blog/sucuri-review-2026) covers a provider-agnostic option that layers on top of either Bluehost or SiteGround.

## Real-World Use Cases

**Personal blog or portfolio (low traffic):** Bluehost's entry plan is sufficient, and the free domain plus lower renewal price make it the more economical pick if you don't expect meaningful traffic growth in year one.

**Small business site with contact forms and a handful of pages:** Either host works.

**WooCommerce store or membership site:** SiteGround is the clear recommendation.

**Agency managing multiple client sites:** SiteGround's Site Tools dashboard, staging environments, and WP-CLI support scale better across many WordPress installs than Bluehost's cPanel-based workflow, though agencies already standardized on cPanel may prefer Bluehost's familiarity.

## Renewal Price: What You'll Actually Pay

The advertised "starting price" for both hosts only applies to the first billing term, usually paid upfront for 12, 24, or 36 months. Here's what renewal actually looks like on a 12-month plan:

| Year | Bluehost (Basic) | SiteGround (StartUp) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Year 1 (promo) | ~$35.40 | ~$35.88 |
| Year 2+ (renewal) | ~$131.88/yr | ~$215.88/yr |

Both hosts require you to actively cancel or downgrade before renewal if you don't want to pay the full rate — neither auto-discounts at renewal without you calling in or using a retention offer. Set a calendar reminder for 30 days before your renewal date regardless of which host you pick.

## Which Should You Choose?

If you're launching your first WordPress site on a tight budget and want the lowest possible entry cost with a free domain, **Bluehost** is a reasonable, well-supported starting point — see our full [Bluehost review](/blog/bluehost-review-2026) for the complete breakdown.

If speed, uptime, and WordPress-specific support quality matter more than shaving a few dollars off the monthly bill — especially for WooCommerce, membership sites, or anything traffic-sensitive — **SiteGround** is the stronger long-term choice.
