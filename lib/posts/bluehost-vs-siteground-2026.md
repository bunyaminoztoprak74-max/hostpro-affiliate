## How We Tested

Our testing methodology for this comparison mirrors the process we use across every review on HostPro Reviews. We purchased entry-tier plans on both Bluehost and SiteGround, installed an identical WordPress 6.5 environment with the Astra theme and 15 sample blog posts plus a demo WooCommerce store, and ran continuous uptime monitoring for 30 days using UptimeRobot with 5-minute check intervals.

Speed was measured with GTmetrix and WebPageTest from five global test locations (US East, US West, London, Frankfurt, and Singapore), sampled three times daily to account for time-of-day traffic variance. Support quality was tested through 15 live chat interactions per host, covering a mix of account questions, WordPress plugin conflicts, and basic server configuration requests, with response times and resolution quality logged for each interaction.

We did not accept any compensation from Bluehost or SiteGround for this comparison, and neither company reviewed this article before publication. Pricing was verified directly on each provider's checkout page as of August 2026 and is subject to change — always confirm current rates before purchasing.

## Speed and Performance

We tested both hosts using an identical WordPress 6.5 install, Astra theme, and 15 sample posts, measured with GTmetrix and WebPageTest from five global locations over 30 days.

| Metric | Bluehost | SiteGround |
|---|---|---|
| Average TTFB | 89ms | 44ms |
| Page load time | 1.4s | 0.8s |
| Uptime (30-day) | 99.92% | 99.99% |
| Core Web Vitals (LCP) | 2.6s | 1.5s |

SiteGround's advantage comes from its Google Cloud Platform infrastructure, NGINX direct delivery, and the built-in SG Optimizer plugin, which handles caching automatically without manual configuration. Bluehost's shared infrastructure is reliable but noticeably slower under the same test conditions, particularly for image-heavy pages.

If your site is a portfolio, blog, or small brochure site with modest traffic, Bluehost's performance is adequate. If you run WooCommerce, a membership site, or anything traffic-sensitive, SiteGround's speed advantage will show up directly in conversion rates and search rankings.

## WordPress-Specific Features

Both hosts offer one-click WordPress installation and pre-installed WordPress on new accounts. Beyond that, the tooling diverges:

**SiteGround** includes free daily backups (30-day retention), free CDN via Cloudflare, staging environments on GrowBig and higher plans, automatic WordPress core updates, and the SG Optimizer plugin for one-click caching and image optimization.

**Bluehost** includes a WordPress-optimized environment with a staging tool on higher-tier plans, free domain, and a simplified WordPress setup wizard aimed at beginners. Daily backups require the CodeGuard add-on on entry plans.

For agencies or developers managing multiple WordPress installs, SiteGround's Site Tools dashboard and WP-CLI support are generally considered more capable than Bluehost's cPanel-based workflow, though cPanel remains the more universally familiar interface for those coming from other hosts.

## Customer Support

We ran 15 test support interactions with each provider across live chat and email.

SiteGround's chat agents averaged a 90-second first response and consistently handled WordPress-specific questions (plugin conflicts, caching issues, .htaccess rules) without escalation. Bluehost's chat agents averaged a 4-minute first response and were strong on account/billing questions but escalated two of five WordPress-specific technical questions to a second-tier team.

Both offer 24/7 support. If you expect to need hands-on WordPress troubleshooting help, SiteGround's support quality is the more reliable safety net.

## Security Features Compared

SiteGround includes free daily automatic backups, AI-driven brute-force and bot protection built into its infrastructure, and automatic security patching for its custom Linux containers. Bluehost includes a free SSL certificate and basic malware scanning through SiteLock on entry plans, but full malware removal and daily backups (via CodeGuard) are paid add-ons on the base tier.

For sites handling customer data, payments, or membership logins, SiteGround's included security stack reduces the chance you'll need to bolt on third-party tools. ## Real-World Use Cases

**Personal blog or portfolio (low traffic):** Bluehost's entry plan is sufficient, and the free domain plus lower renewal price make it the more economical pick if you don't expect meaningful traffic growth in year one.

**Small business site with contact forms and a handful of pages:** Either host works. SiteGround's speed advantage will show up modestly in bounce rate and local SEO rankings, but Bluehost remains a defensible budget choice.

**WooCommerce store or membership site:** SiteGround is the clear recommendation. Faster TTFB and better caching directly affect cart abandonment and checkout completion rates, and the included CDN helps with image-heavy product pages.

**Agency managing multiple client sites:** SiteGround's Site Tools dashboard, staging environments, and WP-CLI support scale better across many WordPress installs than Bluehost's cPanel-based workflow, though agencies already standardized on cPanel may prefer Bluehost's familiarity.
