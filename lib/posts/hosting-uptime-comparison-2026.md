Which hosts publish uptime guarantees, and how do you check them? *Editorial note: HostPro Reviews has not run its own lab tests for this guide. Figures below are arithmetic, vendor-stated, or attributed to a named source.*

## Guarantees are not measurements

A published uptime figure such as 99.9% or 99.99% is a vendor statement. It is useful for comparing commitments, but it tells you what the provider promises, not what you will see in practice.

## What to compare in an uptime guarantee

- **Is it a contractual SLA?** Marketing copy and a signed SLA are different things.
- **What is the remedy?** Most hosts offer service credits, not refunds, and the credit is often small relative to the damage from an outage.
- **What is excluded?** Scheduled maintenance, DDoS attacks and customer-caused issues are commonly excluded.
- **How do you claim?** Many hosts require a request within a set window.
- **Is there a public status page?** Incident history helps you judge how openly a provider communicates.

## Where independent data exists

Participation changes from year to year, so check which hosts and plan tiers were included before drawing conclusions.

## Verify it yourself

Use the money-back window to run a free uptime monitor against your own site. Keep the alert history; it is the evidence you would need for an SLA claim.

## Business impact

Downtime costs depend entirely on your revenue per hour and traffic pattern. To estimate yours, take monthly revenue, divide by roughly 720 hours, and multiply by the annual downtime implied by the uptime figure you are considering. Search engines can also be slow to recrawl a site that returns server errors repeatedly, so frequent outages can have an SEO cost.
