What an uptime SLA actually promises, and how to claim a credit if it is missed. *Editorial note: HostPro Reviews has not run its own lab tests for this guide. Figures below are arithmetic, vendor-stated, or attributed to a named source.*

## What to look for in an SLA

- **Scope:** does it cover the server, the network, or your whole site?
- **Measurement:** who measures uptime, and how?
- **Exclusions:** scheduled maintenance, DDoS attacks, force majeure and customer-caused issues are commonly excluded.
- **Remedy:** credit amount and cap.
- **Claim process:** deadline and evidence required.
- **Plan coverage:** some hosts only offer an SLA on higher plans.

## Green flags and red flags

**Green flags:** a written SLA, a public status page, a clear claim process and visible incident history.

**Red flags:** an uptime claim with no written terms, remedies that require you to prove downtime to the minute, or broad exclusions that swallow the guarantee.

## How to claim an SLA credit

1. Capture timestamped evidence, for example alert emails from a free uptime monitor.
2. Check your host's SLA for the claim window.
3. Open a ticket with dates, times and evidence.
4. Ask the host to confirm the credit in writing.

## Reliability vs price

Higher-priced managed plans often come with stronger commitments and redundancy, but a guarantee is only worth what the remedy and your evidence allow. For revenue-critical sites, consider your own monitoring and a backup plan rather than relying only on an SLA.
